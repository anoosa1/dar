Chapter 2: Tides of the Outer Bay

By noon the wind had shifted southwest, lifting whitecaps past the granite breakwater and rattling the iron louvers of the observatory dome.

On the chart table lay three overlapping tracings of the estuary, each drawn in a different decade, showing how the sandbars drifted slowly like sleeping creatures beneath the brown water.

Mira lifted the brass dividers and stepped them from the headland to the bell buoy, counting leagues in silence while rain ticked steadily against the southern panes.

In the margin of the oldest chart, her grandfather had marked a line of soundings in faded crimson—soundings no modern ferry timetable acknowledged.

“Measure twice when the barometer falls,” he had written along the collar of the ledger, “and trust neither glass nor memory alone.”

Outside, the harbour bell rang twice for slack water. She rolled the tracing paper into its copper cylinder and reached for her oilskin coat.

1. Architecture & Memory Safety

Modern systems runtimes combine zero-cost abstractions with predictable latency under high concurrency workloads.

In this chapter we examine ownership boundaries, task scheduling queues, and lock-free ring buffers in Rust. Every async task owns its state machine on the heap, eliminating hidden thread stacks and minimizing cache misses.

Careful budget enforcement prevents noisy background futures from starving interactive requests sharing the same executor pool.
