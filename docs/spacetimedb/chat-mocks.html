Chat — bridged messaging mocks

A Chat app in the unified frame that is a client for other platforms rather than a network of its own. WhatsApp comes first: Dar links to your account as a companion device (the multi-device protocol, through whatsmeow), the way WhatsApp Web does, so your phone keeps working and Dar receives and sends on its behalf. Discord follows later. Accounts never mix: tabs at the top of the sidebar switch the whole app to one account, so WhatsApp’s chats, views, unread counts, notifications and media folder stay apart from Discord’s. Two directions differ in where the chat list lives; the account tabs, conversation, details, linking and states are shared.

  • Separate accounts: one tab per linked account under New chat, with its unread count; the selected tab scopes everything below it. Each platform keeps its own shape: WhatsApp has Chats, Unread, Groups and Archived; Discord (C) has direct messages and servers with channels. Alternative D makes each platform its own rail app instead.
  • A · Inbox and conversation: the sidebar holds the account tabs and that account’s views; the chat list is its own sheet and the conversation fills the rest. Chat info opens as a right sheet. Closest to the Files/Contacts/News pattern.
  • B · Conversation first: the chat list moves into the sidebar under the account tabs (pinned and recent, view chips on top, sync status at the bottom), as Agent does with threads, so the conversation gets the width.
  • Shared: read ticks, replies, reactions, edits, voice notes, photos and documents map to WhatsApp’s own features; media can be saved into a Files folder and a chat can be linked to a Dar contact.
  • Linking: Link an account opens the right editing pane with a QR code on desktop and a pairing code on phones, an account name and whether to import recent history.
  • States: phone offline, reconnecting and unlinked banners, failed sends with Retry, deleted and unsupported messages (polls, calls) and a pending media download.
  • Open questions: whatsmeow is a Go library, so it needs a bridge process under Cordis next to the Node runtime; where message history and media live (SpacetimeDB rows, blobs, a Files folder) and how much to import; whether search covers message text. Discord forbids automating a personal account, so Discord support means either a bot (only servers that add it, no personal DMs) or accepting that risk.
